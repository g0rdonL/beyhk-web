BX-48 at a Glance: Specs, Parts & Essentials

2026-11-13 · BeyHK

BX-48 specs, part pairings and honest buying notes sit in one place here, because Random Booster Vol. 9 revives five blades that Hong Kong players could previously only reach through Japanese events, app prizes, B4 stores, a CoroCoro lottery or a manga bundle. This guide covers what matters before you spend: the five revival Beys and their new ratchet and bit pairings, measured blade weights, what actually changed from the originals, the real price, and how the November 2026 lottery restock reset the hunt. A pick list by build type and answers to the questions Hong Kong players ask most close it out.

Key takeaways

  • - BX-48 Random Booster Vol. 9, subtitled Limited Revival, holds one of five metal coat Beys per box at a ¥1,600 Japanese MSRP and launched in Japan on February 14, 2026.
  • - All five blades first shipped as limited releases through events, app prizes, the B4 store, a CoroCoro lottery or a manga special edition, and the ratchet and bit pairings are new for this run.
  • - Cobalt Dragoon 9-80F (Metal Coat: Black) is the heaviest pull at a measured 37.7 g and needs a left-spin launcher.
  • - Takara Tomy does not publish pull ratios for the five Beys, and the November 2026 Takara Tomy Mall lottery restock closed entries on November 3.

What is the BX-48 Random Booster Vol. 9?

Random Booster Vol. 9 (BX-48) is a Takara Tomy Beyblade X random booster released in Japan on February 14, 2026 at ¥1,600 per box, tax included. Each box contains one of five metal coat revival Beys, and every blade in the run first shipped as a limited release through events, the app, B4 stores or print media.

On the official BX-48 product page, Takara Tomy confirms the five-Bey lineup, the price and two hardware caveats: a Beyblade X stadium and launcher are required to battle, and Cobalt Dragoon needs a separate left-spin launcher. Each box holds one complete Bey (blade, ratchet and bit) plus the manual and a Bey code card, per Dengeki Hobby Web's product listing.

The subtitle matters: this is a limited revival, not a standard wave. Every blade in the box is a reissue of a Japan-only exclusive color, which is why the lineup reads like a greatest-hits collection rather than a debut.

Why the limited revival matters for Hong Kong players

Four of the five blades in this box were, until February 2026, effectively unavailable in Hong Kong. B4 stores are a Japan-only network of Beyblade X retail partners, and the original metal coat Shark Edge shipped only through that channel in March 2024. The app prize blades were tied to Japan's Rare Bey Get Battle campaigns, and the red Dran Buster came out of a CoroCoro lottery and a manga special edition. None of those channels helps a Hong Kong buyer standing in a local toy shop.

The revival changes that: the same blade colors now arrive through normal retail and importer stock, at the standard booster price, with new part pairings attached. For a Hong Kong player building a tournament deck, that turns a collection piece into usable equipment. If you are new to the generation first, our Beyblade X starter guide covers the stadium, launcher and launch basics this set assumes.

The five BX-48 Beys: every part pairing

Five units fill the run, numbered BX-48-01 through BX-48-05. Takara Tomy paired each revival blade with ratchet and bit combinations built specifically for this booster, and Obake Blader's lineup breakdown traces each blade back to its original limited release. Blade weights below come from Kyogiken's measured weight tables, which log Japanese community measurements per production run.

01 Cobalt Dragoon 9-80F (Metal Coat: Black): the headliner

Cobalt Dragoon returns as a left-spin attack blade and the heaviest in the box at a measured 37.7 g, with the thick, layered profile that made the original a headline blade. Hamusuke's unboxing notes describe a four-layer attack blade built to knock opponents away, paired here with the 9-80 ratchet: nine small teeth at a tall 8.0 mm. The F (Flat) bit sends the top racing outward after launch, which is exactly where the risk sits, because an overpowered launch can self-KO into an Xtreme finish for your opponent. Remember the hardware note: this Bey needs a left-spin launcher, and its blade revived from the event-limited Cobalt Dragoon 2-60C (Metal Coat: Black).

02 Shark Edge 4-70E (Metal Coat: Blue): the revised classic

Shark Edge is a right-spin attack blade with two large upper fins, and its revival color first appeared as the B4 store limited Shark Edge 5-60GF (Metal Coat: Blue), a ¥1,600 Japan-only store exclusive released on March 23, 2024. The BX-48 version weighs a measured 34.3 g and pairs the blade with the 4-70 ratchet (four teeth at 7.0 mm) and the E (Elevate) bit, a popular balance choice. One production detail is worth knowing before you mix parts: the revised Elevate socket on this copy is slightly thicker than the 2024 original, a fit fix for a bit long known for easier removal, which Hamusuke's Shark Edge review documents.

03 Mammoth Tusk 7-60S (Metal Coat: Black): the parts pick

Mammoth Tusk is the lightest blade here at a measured 32.3 g, a balance-type design with four streamlined smash blades made for tilting opponents rather than blasting them. The pairing is where the value sits: the 7-60 ratchet (seven teeth at 6.0 mm) is one of the most popular ratchets in the game, and the S (Spike) bit is a classic pointed defense tip. The blade revived from a Mammoth Tusk 2-80E (Metal Coat: Black) that moved through an event advance sale and then the app's Rare Bey Get Battle campaign, and reviewers treat this unit primarily as a parts pull for exactly that reason.

04 Hells Scythe 3-85GB (Metal Coat: Gold): the stamina sleeper

Hells Scythe runs a right-spin balance profile on four smash blades, and the metal coat lifts its published stats above the clear version, with Kyogiken's part sheets logging attack 35, defense 40 and stamina 40 against 30/35/35 for the standard blade. The coat also adds weight: this copy measures 33.5 g against about 32.7 g clear.

The 3-85 ratchet (three teeth at 8.5 mm) first shipped in the UX-10 Customize Set U, which we covered in our UX-10 parts guide, and the GB (Gear Ball) bit under it is a stamina-type ball tip with a gear ring, per Gear Ball's spec entry on BeyGear. The gold blade revived from an app prize Hells Scythe 4-60T (Metal Coat: Gold).

05 Dran Buster 2-80Q (Metal Coat: Red): the lottery relic

Dran Buster is a right-spin attack blade built around single-strike impact, weighing a measured 36.5 g. Its revival color has the most unusual origin in the run: the red blade shipped as a CoroCoro Comic lottery prize in the May 2024 issue and as the bonus of the manga's full-color volume 1 special edition, so most players never held one. The 2-80 ratchet (two teeth at 8.0 mm) carries a shape that parries attacks on right spin, and the Q (Quake) bit's diagonally cut tip sends the top bouncing across the stadium in irregular lines.

What changed from the original limited releases

Three things separate a BX-48 unit from the limited release it revives. First, the blades keep their metal coat colors but carry minor design adjustments, which Takara Tomy flags on the product page. Second, every ratchet and bit pairing here is new for this run: the parts were recombined specifically for this booster rather than copied from the original boxes. Third, production details moved: the Shark Edge copy carries a slightly thicker Elevate socket than its 2024 ancestor, and the metal coat adds about a gram of blade weight on Hells Scythe (33.5 g measured against 32.7 g for the clear version). None of that changes what the blade is for, but it does mean a BX-48 pull is not a parts-identical twin of the original box.

BX-48 price and availability in Hong Kong

Japanese MSRP is ¥1,600 per box, tax included, unchanged from launch day. The February run sold through standard retail quickly, and Takara Tomy answered with a Takara Tomy Mall lottery restock in November 2026: entries closed at 23:59 on November 3, with boxes shipping from mid-November. That window is now shut, so the realistic paths from Hong Kong are the next lottery round (watch the official page), importer shops, or the secondary market, where singles trade above box price once a Bey is in demand.

Before you buy blind, apply the same box math we used for CX-17: price the single you actually want against the cost of pulling it from boxes. If a loose Cobalt Dragoon costs less than two or three boxes on the secondary market, the single usually wins.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the BX-48 Random Booster cost?

The Japanese MSRP is ¥1,600 per box, tax included. Retail stock now moves through lottery rounds rather than shelves, so importer and secondary market prices run higher for in-demand Beys. The November 2026 Takara Tomy Mall lottery closed entries on November 3, so check the official product page for the next window before paying resale prices.

What are the odds of pulling each BX-48 Bey?

Takara Tomy lists five possible Beys and does not publish per-Bey ratios, so treat any box as a one-in-five chance at best rather than a promise. Budget at least five boxes if you want the full set, expect duplicates, and trade spares with local players; trading usually beats buying blind boxes until the last slot drops.

Does the BX-48 include a launcher or stadium?

No. The box holds one complete Bey (blade, ratchet and bit) plus the manual and a Bey code card. You need a Beyblade X stadium and a launcher, both sold separately, and Cobalt Dragoon specifically requires a left-spin launcher, an extra purchase if you only own the standard ripper.

Which BX-48 pull is the best for your deck?

It depends on the build. Cobalt Dragoon brings maximum attack weight, Mammoth Tusk carries the popular 7-60 ratchet and Spike bit, Hells Scythe assembles a stamina kit, Shark Edge supplies the revised Elevate bit, and Dran Buster delivers heavy single-strike offense. For parts value, reviewers point at Mammoth Tusk and Shark Edge first.

Are BX-48 blades identical to the original limited versions?

Not exactly. Blades keep their metal coat colors with minor design adjustments, while every ratchet and bit pairing is new for this run. The Shark Edge copy also carries a slightly thicker Elevate socket than the 2024 original, a small revision worth knowing before you swap bits between boxes.

Is the BX-48 safe to mix with older Burst Beys?

No, and you should not try it. Takara Tomy warns that Beyblade X tops can injure players or break Burst generation parts when the series are mixed, because the systems are not compatible. Battle in a proper stadium, check bits for cracks after hard fights, and expect the metal coat paint to wear with use.
