Beyblade X Beginner Guide: How to Start Playing in Hong Kong

2026-11-16 · Gordon Lee

New to Beyblade X? This guide covers what a new player in Hong Kong needs to know: what to buy, where to find a match, how your first match works, and how to get on the BeyHK ladder. You don’t need to know it all first; after your first match you’ll know what to do next.

What you need

  • A Starter: Starters are the only tier that comes with a launcher. Random Boosters are parts only, so a Booster on its own won’t get you playing.
  • A Beyblade X stadium: you need the Xtreme Line rail to trigger an Xtreme Dash. If you play at a shop there is usually a stadium already, so you don’t have to buy one straight away.
  • The part codes: Dran Sword 3-60F, for example, is the Dran Sword blade on a ratchet with 3 contact points and 6.0 mm height, with a Flat bit.

For parts, product lines and codes in detail, read Inside Beyblade X: Specs, Components & Design Details. The BX (Basic) line is the safest place to start, and its parts work with UX and CX later.

How scoring works

Official matches go to 4 points:

  • Xtreme Finish: 3 points
  • Over Finish (out of the stadium): 2 points
  • Burst Finish: 2 points
  • Spin Finish (the other top stops first): 1 point

In 3-on-3 battles each player brings three tops, and no part may appear more than once across the three.

Where to find a match

The BeyHK app has a map of shops and venues in Hong Kong. Every shop has its own chat room, and there is also a Hong Kong-wide room and one for every tier. The simplest way in:

  1. Find a shop or venue near you on the map.
  2. Join its chat room and set up your first match.
  3. Challenge someone straight from chat; once they accept, the match log opens.

Chat has block, report, a profanity filter and moderators.

Your first match: log it, your opponent confirms

After you play, enter the score in the app: first to 4, first to 3 or a single round, round by round if you like (spin, over, burst and Xtreme finishes). Then your opponent confirms:

  • They scan your QR code on the spot, or confirm later from a push notification.
  • They have 48 hours; after that the match expires.
  • A rating only moves once the opponent confirms, so every match on the ladder is one both players agreed to.

Five matches to the ladder

Your first 5 confirmed matches are placement matches, which set your starting rank and tier. After them you appear on the Hong Kong ladder in one of four tiers (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum), with district ladders too. BeyHK uses Glicko-2: besides a rating it tracks a deviation (±), and the more you play, the smaller the deviation and the more accurate your rank. See how the ranking works.

Tournaments are separate from the ladder: single-elimination and Swiss events that shops and organizers run in the app never touch the ranking.

BeyHK is for players aged 13 and over. A player under 13 needs a parent to set up a managed account with the parent’s own email; a guardian PIN then decides whether chat, direct messages, friends, photos and the meet-up check are on. See Parents & safety.

FAQ

What does a code like 3-60F mean?

The Ratchet numbers give the number of locking points and the height in tenths of a millimetre; the trailing letter is the Bit. 3-60F means a Ratchet with 3 locking points, 6.0 mm high, paired with a Flat tip [5]. The taller the height number, the higher the top sits; the more locking points, the firmer the lock and the better the burst resistance.

Which Beyblade X product line should a beginner buy?

Start with BX, the Basic Line. It is the cheapest complete system, uses the standard three-part setup, and its parts are fully interchangeable with the other lines [5]. Look into CX only when you want deep customisation.

How much does a Beyblade X Starter cost?

On Takara Tomy's Japanese line, Starters run ¥1,980 to ¥2,750 depending on the series and box size, and each includes a launcher. Random Boosters are about ¥1,400 to ¥1,600, but contain parts only [6].

Is Beyblade X compatible with older Beyblade generations?

No. Official play only recognises Beyblade X tops, launchers and stadiums; Burst, Metal Fight and first-generation products are all banned at official events [7].

Can you mix parts from different Beyblade X lines?

Yes. Blades, Ratchets, Bits, launchers and stadiums all work across BX, UX and CX; only some of the newer tops integrate the Ratchet into the Blade or Bit [3]. One deck-building rule to remember: in official 3-on-3 play, the same part may not be reused across your three Beys [7].

What is Xtreme Dash?

It is the burst of acceleration triggered when a Bit's gear meshes with the stadium's Xtreme Line rail. The mesh is mechanical, so it depends on the Bit's design rather than launch power alone [4].

What are the risks of buying Beyblade X online?

The main risks are counterfeits and scalper prices. Takara Tomy has published a notice on unofficial products and has taken action against brand-related fraud [8]. Purchase from licensed retailers; if a price appears too good to be true, consider it a warning sign.

Conclusion

The takeaway that matters most for beginners: read the part code before you buy, because the Ratchet height and the Bit letter already tell you how the top will move before you ever launch it. What you can do right now is pick a BX Starter that suits your style — Flat Bit for attack, Needle or Ball for defence and stamina — then battle your friends with the official 4-point scoring. And when you want to record your matches and track which combos win the most, the BeyHK app logs every battle and ranks you against Hong Kong players, so your part choices are backed by real data: beyhk.sh
