Inside cx-10: Specs, Components & Design Details

2026-11-09 · BeyHK

cx-10 is the product code for the Wolf Hunt F0-60DB booster, a stamina-type top in Takara Tomy's Beyblade X Custom Line that went on sale in Japan on 1 November 2025 at ¥1,700 including tax. This guide breaks down every stock part, the community part ratings, what the top actually does in battle, the real price in Japan and Hong Kong, and the buying traps (launchers, fakes, Hasbro numbering) that the product listings skip. If you are deciding whether Wolf Hunt earns a slot in your deck, the tables and decision framework below give you the answer in about ten minutes.

What is cx-10?

CX-10 is the product code for Booster Wolf Hunt F0-60DB, a stamina-type, right-spin booster in Takara Tomy's Beyblade X Custom Line. It launched in Japan on 1 November 2025 at ¥1,700 tax included, and its blade splits into three swappable pieces for customisation, per Takara Tomy's official CX-10 page.

That single sentence hides three things new buyers trip over, so let us unpack them.

The code vs the name. "CX-10" is the SKU, not the top's name. The top is called Wolf Hunt, its full stock notation is F0-60DB, and the blade splits into a Wolf Lock Chip, a Hunt Main Blade and a Free Assist Blade. When a friend says "cx-10" and a listing says "Wolf Hunt F0-60DB", they are describing the same product. Beyblade X parts guide In Chinese-speaking markets including Hong Kong, Takara Tomy Asia sells the same item as 戰狼狩獵 (meaning "war wolf hunt"), so under that name in local shops and on Carousell you are looking at the identical mould.

The line. CX stands for Custom Line, the third Beyblade X parts system after the Basic Line (BX) and Unique Line (UX). The Custom Line keeps the Ratchet and Bit but splits the Blade itself into three separate pieces, so a complete CX combo is five parts in total: Lock Chip, Main Blade, Assist Blade, Ratchet and Bit, as the Monkee Mods CX system guide explains. CX-01 Dran Brave opened the line in March 2025, and CX-10 is the tenth numbered Japan-market release.

The format. "Booster" on the box means the top only. The official product page states plainly that this product requires a separately sold Beyblade X stadium and launcher. Budget for those if this is your first bey.

How the code decodes: F0-60DB

Beyblade X stock notations read as Blade-Ratchet-Bit:

Code Meaning
F Hunt Main Blade (code F)
0 0 ratchet (no protrusions)
60 6.0 mm ratchet height
DB Disc Ball bit

So "F0-60DB" tells you the whole stock combo before you open the box. Once you can read this line, every Beyblade X product code stops being alphabet soup.

Why cx-10 matters: the stamina role and the 3-part blade

The five-part CX stack changes what you can tune

In BX and UX tops, the blade is one fixed unit; its weight and contact profile are locked the moment you pick it. CX breaks that unit apart. The Main Blade is the metal-and-plastic ring that carries most of the mass and does most of the hitting; the Lock Chip and Assist Blade sit around it and are swappable independently, which is where the Custom name comes from. Practically, that means the same Hunt main blade can run defensive, balanced or endurance-tuned profiles depending on which chip and assist blade you pair it with, and parts can move between CX products freely.

Wolf Hunt's job in the current meta

Wolf Hunt is built to deflect hits, preserve spin and grind opponents down through repeated contact. The Mall of Toys hands-on review describes a top that weathers aggressive strikes early, then outlasts opponents in marathon exchanges, with the Disc Ball bit keeping it centred and stable even after hard contact. The Monkee Mods product guide frames its trade-off honestly: a high-impact hit, a burst or a stadium exit can still end the battle before endurance matters. Stamina wins long fights; it does not win the first three seconds. battle types explainer

Community threads bear both halves out. The recurring questions on r/BeybladeX about Wolf Hunt are "any good combo?" and "Wolf Hunt or Silver Wolf?", which tells you where the top actually sits: a respected endurance platform and parts donor rather than an instant tournament boss.

Where it fits among the CX releases so far

The Custom Line launched with CX-01 Dran Brave in March 2025, and Japan-market releases have marched through the CX numbering (starters, boosters, random boosters, deck sets) since. Wolf Hunt's job in that sequence is to be the line's first pure stamina flagship, the top you buy when you want to learn endurance play or harvest a free-spinning assist blade and a top-tier bit for your own builds.

The five stock parts

The part breakdown and the community catalogue ratings below are from the Monkee Mods product guide, which labels them as relative part ratings from community catalogues, not measured win rates.

Lock Chip Wolf Identification piece around the main blade; no separate numerical contribution Not rated
Main Blade Hunt Primary contact surface and most of the mass; circular profile built to deflect 20 / 10 / 30
Assist Blade Free Free-spinning piece that absorbs and redirects impact, cutting spin loss 5 / 20 / 25
Ratchet 0-60 No protrusions, 6.0 mm height; smooth base reduces energy loss in collisions 3 / 14 / 13

Read together, the stock build is exactly what the box promises: nothing in the parts list points at attack, and the bit's Stamina 55 is the highest single number in the combo. The free-spinning assist blade is the special sauce, because impact energy that would normally dump spin into an opponent instead spins through the free piece.

What the ratings do and do not tell you

Those numbers describe parts, not results. The same guide is explicit that they are "neither measured win rates nor a promise that the stock combination will beat a particular opponent". Stadium, launch power, opponent match-up and part wear all move the outcome. Treat the table as a map of intent: it tells you what a part is for, and your own repeated testing tells you how it behaves in your hands.

How to test and combo cx-10 properly

You unboxed it, you have a launcher and a stadium. Here is the process the community guides converge on.

  1. Stock first. Run the F0-60DB build as-is for at least ten launches against the same opponent or the same launch routine. You are calibrating your hands before you judge the top.
  2. Count finishes, not vibes. Record survival, burst, knockout and extreme-finish results per set. Stamina types win on survival; if you are getting knocked out repeatedly, the problem may be launch angle rather than parts.
  3. Change one part at a time. Swap the bit first (Disc Ball to Ball or Needle changes the ride height and stability profile dramatically), test ten launches, then consider the ratchet. Changing blade, ratchet and bit simultaneously tells you nothing.
  4. Borrow from the CX pool. The Hunt main blade pairs with other lock chips and assist blades from any CX release, and the Disc Ball bit drops into any X-line ratchet. This is the whole point of the Custom Line. CX parts compatibility
  5. Log everything. Write down each configuration and its results. After three sessions you will have a personal win table that beats any online tier list for your local meta.

The official Beyblade X manual hub hosts the current product manuals and safety guidance; it is worth a skim before your first launch, particularly the stadium-safety points about keeping faces and hands clear.

Two combos worth trying first

  • Endurance benchmark: keep Hunt + Free but swap the ratchet to a taller 7-60 style unit if you own one; the extra height helps the free blade deflect low hits.
  • Recoil control: Wolf lock chip with a heavier defensive assist blade turns the build into a wall that leans on the Disc Ball's self-correction, good practice against the attack-types your friends probably run.

Treat both as starting points for your own table, not netdeck answers. The same CX system guide makes the point: with five-part combos, the combination space is so large that first-hand testing beats borrowed configurations.

How to get started with cx-10

  1. Buy the booster plus the essentials. CX-10 itself, one Beyblade X launcher and a stadium. In Hong Kong the official Takara Tomy Asia mall carries the full X line including launchers and stadiums, and the official Asia mall listing shows the local suggested retail.
  2. Check the sticker before paying. Takara Tomy products carry the JP/EU/Asia distribution stickers; fakes often skip the barcode sheet or misspell the notation on the back.
  3. Assemble by the manual. Lock chip, main blade, assist blade, ratchet, bit, in that order, clicked firmly.
  4. Launch low and smooth. Stamina tops punish wild launches. Consistency first, power later.
  5. Take it to the local scene. Once the stock build feels familiar, bring it to a meetup; real opponents teach you more in one evening than a week of solo launches. Hong Kong Beyblade X community

Frequently asked questions

Does CX-10 include a launcher?

No. CX-10 is a booster, and Takara Tomy's official product page states that a separately sold Beyblade X stadium and launcher are required. Budget for both if this is your first top; the official Asia mall lists launchers and stadiums alongside the boosters.

How much does cx-10 cost?

The official Japan price is ¥1,700 with tax on Takara Tomy's CX-10 product page (beyblade.takaratomy.co.jp, checked November 2026). In Hong Kong, the official Takara Tomy Asia mall lists it at HKD $79.90 suggested retail as 戰狼狩獵. Street prices vary with stock; the two official listings are your reference points.

Is Wolf Hunt actually good, or just hype?

It is a genuinely strong stamina platform with a real weakness: fast kills. The stock build excels at long, grinding exchanges and recovers from hits that would wobble other tops out, but a clean attack hit or a burst ends the fight early. Community discussion has cooled from launch hype, which usually means the top has settled into its true role: parts donor and endurance specialist rather than everything-beater.

What is the difference between cx-10 and the Silver Wolf bey?

Silver Wolf is a different product line entry (and a different lock chip); Wolf Hunt is the CX-10 booster with the Hunt main blade and Free assist blade. They share the wolf theme and the anime character link, which is exactly why the comparison comes up so often in community threads. They play differently: check the stock notation of each before assuming they are interchangeable.

Can I mix cx-10 parts with BX and UX tops?

The ratchet and bit: yes, all X-line ratchets and bits are cross-compatible. The blade parts: only within the Custom Line. The three-piece blade stack (lock chip, main blade, assist blade) exists only on CX tops, so Hunt, Free and Wolf cannot mount on a BX or UX blade unit. That is the main reason CX buyers end up wanting at least one more CX release.

Is there a fake cx-10 risk when buying in Hong Kong?

Counterfeit beyblades circulate on marketplaces and second-hand platforms everywhere, Hong Kong included. Buy from the official Takara Tomy Asia mall or an authorised retailer, check the product sticker and notation printing, and be sceptical of prices far below the official suggested retail. The mould quality on fakes genuinely changes how the top battles, so the cheap listing usually is not a bargain.

What should I buy with cx-10 for a first setup?

A launcher and a stadium, in that order; the booster is unplayable without them. If a friend already owns a stadium, a shared one works for casual testing. Add a second top with a different battle type (any cheap attack-type booster) so you can run real match-ups instead of mirror matches, then bring both to your local scene and start logging results.
