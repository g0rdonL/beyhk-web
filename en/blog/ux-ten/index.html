UX-10 101: Specs, Parts & What Matters

2026-11-12 · BeyHK

ux-10 specs start with the box itself: the UX-10 Customize Set U is a Takara Tomy Beyblade X parts set released in Japan on November 2, 2024 for 5,700 yen with tax, packing four blades, three ratchets and six bits that combine into 72 different builds, according to Takara Tomy's official product page. This guide breaks down what is inside, what each part actually does, how much it costs in Hong Kong, and the honest pros and cons, so you know exactly what you are getting before you buy.

  • The set contains 13 parts total: four blades, three ratchets and six bits, enough for 72 combinations.
  • It is the only mid-2024 set that reprints Tyranno Beat and Hells Hammer with official Metal Coat plating, in red and blue respectively.
  • The 3-85 ratchet inside sets a fixed burst resistance of 30 no matter which bit you pair it with, as measured in Japanese part databases.
  • In Hong Kong the set has been listed around HK$499 by local importers, well above its 5,700 yen Japanese retail price.
  • For 3-on-3 tournaments, this one box covers most of the duplicate-part rule: three complete Beys with no part shared between them.

What is the UX-10?

The UX-10 Customize Set U is a Takara Tomy Beyblade X customization set released in Japan on November 2, 2024 for 5,700 yen with tax, containing four blades, three ratchets and six bits. Officially it is billed as a strong parts set for 72-way customization, and it is the tenth release in the UX "Unique Line" that started with UX-01 in July 2024, as tracked by the Japanese competition toy research database.

The "U" stands for Unique, the UX line's own name. Unique Line blades shift more metal outward than the standard Beyblade X line, which is why the four blades here feel heavier in the hand than their BX-era counterparts. Unlike a starter set, no launcher or stadium is included, so you need your own BX-series launcher and Beyblade X stadium to battle with it, a requirement Takara Tomy states directly on the product page.

What is inside the box

The box holds 13 parts in fixed colourways, no random packs and no duplicates:

Part type In this set Notes
Blades (4) Knight Mail, Ptero Swing, Tyranno Beat (Metal Coat: Red), Hells Hammer (Metal Coat: Blue) Knight Mail and Ptero Swing are new Unique Line molds; the two Metal Coats are first reprints
Ratchets (3) 3-85, 7-70, 1-60 3-85 and 7-70 are new heights; 1-60 is a reprint of the common low ratchet
Bits (6) Bound Spike (BS), Rubber Accel (RA), Ball (B), Rush (R), Point (P), Metal Needle (MN) BS and RA are new; the other four are popular reprints

The set assembles into four featured stock combos per the part list cross-checked by monkeemods: Knight Mail 3-85BS, Tyranno Beat 1-60RA (Metal Coat: Red), Ptero Swing 7-70B, and Hells Hammer with the remaining bits. Japanese reviewer Hamusuke's unboxing confirms the Metal Coat Tyranno Beat is red and the Hells Hammer is blue, both first reprints with plating.

Why this set matters for Hong Kong players

Hong Kong has no Takara Tomy flagship store, so big parts sets arrive through importers months after the Japan date, usually at a premium. The set's Japanese price was 5,700 yen with tax in November 2024; what that turns into in Hong Kong dollars is covered in the price section below. Knowing the original 5,700 yen number matters because it tells you how much of the local price is import margin, and whether a sale is actually a deal.

The second reason is tournament practical. The official BEYBLADE X Regulations (6th edition) require that in a 3-on-3 deck you cannot use the same part twice across your three Beys, even in a different colour. One box of UX-10 does not build a full legal deck by itself, but it supplies the rare middle layer: three ratchet heights and six distinct bits are exactly the parts most players run short of when assembling three non-overlapping combos.

Third, the meta parts. Tyranno Beat with a red Metal Coat and the Rubber Accel bit were both strong parts in Japanese tournament circles through 2025, and community reviewers keep calling this the best-value single purchase of the UX line for exactly that reason.

ux-10 specs: every part measured

Every number below comes from Japanese part databases and the official product page. Blade weights vary a little by production run, so treat them as ranges.

Blade weight comparison

The four blades differ most in weight, and weight decides how they trade hits:

Blade Weight range Role Metal Coat in this set
1 Knight Mail: 36.4 to 36.9 g Round defense blade with three internal steel balls that shift vertically to absorb hits, as the Beyblade X Database describes none
2 Tyranno Beat: 36.7 to 37.1 g Aggressive eight-sided attack blade with sharp contact points Red
3 Ptero Swing: 34.2 to 34.5 g Mid-weight balance blade with upper/lower difference none
4 Hells Hammer: 32.8 to 33.2 g Three-sided hammer blade, lighter than the rest Blue

Weight ranges reflect community measurements compiled in the Beyblade X Database, which lists each blade on its own part page.

The 3-85 ratchet: the special case

The 3-85 ratchet is the only triple-blade ratchet in the set and the tallest here. Two facts make it unusual. First, per the obakeblader part breakdown, the 3-85 locks burst resistance at 30 regardless of which bit it pairs with, unlike other ratchets whose burst resistance follows the bit. Second, it was the most suspected part when players compared it to the standard 3-60, because the taller gap leaves more blade overhang.

That is also why the Knight Mail 3-85BS stock combo works: the Bound Spike bit's rubber base absorbs vertical shock while the tall ratchet keeps the blade away from the opponent's low hits. The database entry for 3-85 notes the height makes it harder to hit cleanly, at the cost of a slightly higher centre of gravity.

Bits: six ways to move

The six bits cover the three standard families. Attack: Rush and Rubber Accel, where RA swaps a hard tip for rubber so it grips the stadium floor as it accelerates. Defense: Ball and Bound Spike; the Japanese part entry for Bound Spike documents its bouncing rubber tip on a flat base, a shape built to absorb vertical shock. Stamina: Point and Metal Needle, with MN the sharper, faster-wearing needle of the two.

How the 72 combinations actually work

Four blades times three ratchets times six bits is 4 × 3 × 6 = 72, which is where Takara Tomy's number comes from. In practice you will only assemble a handful, so here is a decision table for the combos most Hong Kong tournament players start with.

# Combo Style Best for
1 Knight Mail 3-85BS Defense Official stock combo; tall, bouncy, hard to knock out; good near the Over Zone pocket
2 Tyranno Beat 1-60RA (Red) Attack Low, aggressive, rubber grip for the Xtreme Line; high risk, high reward
3 Ptero Swing 7-70B Balance Mid height with the safest bit family; forgiving for newer players
4 Hells Hammer + MN or P Stamina Light blade with a needle tip for long spin battles; weakest blade but decent tips

The honest read: the two reprints (Tyranno Beat red, Hells Hammer blue) matter most to competitive players who missed them, while Knight Mail is the reason a newcomer should pick this over a random booster. Random boosters gamble; this set guarantees all 13 parts.

Price in Hong Kong and where to buy

Japanese retail was 5,700 yen with tax on November 2, 2024, and no later reprint has reset that price. Two years on, Hong Kong importers list it around HK$499. At typical exchange rates that is roughly 60 percent above the Japanese retail price converted, which is a normal premium for an out-of-production set that is no longer being reprinted.

Local availability changes fast: T CLUB listed it at HK$269 on pre-order in mid-2026 before selling out, and Toys "R" Us Hong Kong ran an online pre-order with July 2026 shipping that has since closed. Both listings are gone at the time of writing, so the realistic options are remaining importer stock around HK$499, the second-hand market, or waiting for a reprint wave. You can check current detailed prices on the importer product pages themselves, since stock and pricing move weekly.

Pros, cons and limitations

The table below is the honest summary after two years on the market:

Pros Cons
All 13 parts guaranteed, no random booster gambling No launcher or stadium included; you need both already
First Metal Coat reprints of Tyranno Beat (red) and Hells Hammer (blue) Import premium in HK: around HK$499 vs 5,700 yen Japan retail
Covers most of the 3-on-3 duplicate-part rule for ratchets and bits 3-85 ratchet locks burst resistance at 30, which some players dislike
Knight Mail is a genuinely new Unique Line blade, not a reprint Hells Hammer at about 33 g is outclassed by newer CX-line blades

One more limitation worth stating plainly: the set is two years old. Japanese meta lists from 2026 rate newer CX-line blades above everything in this box for raw attack, so competitive-first players buy this for the ratchets, bits and Metal Coats, not for a tournament-winning blade.

How to get started with the set

Getting value from it takes one evening. Work down this list:

  1. Assemble all four featured stock combos first, one at a time, and launch each five times in your stadium to feel the difference in height and grip.
  2. Pick the one that matches your style: Knight Mail 3-85BS if you like defense, Tyranno Beat 1-60RA if you want speed on the Xtreme Line.
  3. Swap exactly one part and launch again; changing one variable at a time is the fastest way to learn what ratchets and bits actually do.
  4. Before a 3-on-3 event, line up your three Beys and confirm no part repeats across them, per the official regulation covered above.
  5. Register the results of your test battles on the Hong Kong ranking so the numbers, not the feelings, decide which combo stays in your deck.

Frequently asked questions

What does the UX-10 set include?

It includes four blades (Knight Mail, Ptero Swing, Tyranno Beat Metal Coat Red, Hells Hammer Metal Coat Blue), three ratchets (3-85, 7-70, 1-60) and six bits (Bound Spike, Rubber Accel, Ball, Rush, Point, Metal Needle). That is 13 parts total, combining into 72 different Beyblade X builds. No launcher, grip or stadium is included, so those must come from another set you already own.

How much does the UX-10 cost in Hong Kong?

Hong Kong importers list it at around HK$499 as of November 2026, against its 5,700 yen Japanese retail price from November 2, 2024. Earlier 2026 pre-orders ran lower, with T CLUB at HK$269 before selling out. Stock moves weekly, so check the current figure on importer product pages such as TradeLazy before buying, and compare against the second-hand market for a used set.

Is the UX-10 still worth buying in 2026?

Yes, for the parts, not the meta. Its ratchets (3-85, 7-70), bits (Bound Spike, Rubber Accel) and the Metal Coat reprints remain useful in 2026 decks, and the 3-on-3 duplicate-part rule makes spare ratchets and bits permanently valuable. What it will not give you is a top-tier attack blade by current standards; newer CX-line releases outclass everything in the box for raw hitting power.

What is the best combo from this set?

Knight Mail 3-85BS is the safest answer: the tall ratchet keeps the round blade away from low hits while the Bound Spike bit bounces off and absorbs vertical shock, which is exactly the pairing Takara Tomy designed it for. For attack players, Tyranno Beat 1-60RA uses the rubber tip to grip the Xtreme Line for extreme dashes, at the cost of consistency. Start with Knight Mail, then adjust one part at a time.

Does the UX-10 come with a launcher?

No. The UX-10 is a parts set only: four blades, three ratchets and six bits. Takara Tomy's product page states you need the separately sold Beyblade X stadium and launcher to battle. If you own any BX starter set, your existing launcher works fine, since the launcher standard is shared across the whole line.

Can the 3-85 ratchet burst easily?

The 3-85 ratchet holds a fixed burst resistance of 30 no matter which bit you use, per Japanese part measurements. That is low compared with pairs like 1-60 plus Point, so 3-85 builds give up some burst safety in exchange for height and a three-blade contact profile. The Knight Mail 3-85BS combo offsets this with the Bound Spike bit's rubber damping, but expect less burst resistance overall.

Is the UX-10 legal in official tournaments?

Yes. Every part in the set is an official Takara Tomy Beyblade X release, and the regulations only require official, unmodified parts. The one rule that matters is the 3-on-3 deck check: you cannot use the same part in two of your three Beys, even in a different colour. The set's three ratchets and six distinct bits make it much easier to build three legal, non-overlapping combos from one purchase.
