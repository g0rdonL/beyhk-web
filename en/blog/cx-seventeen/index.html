CX-17 at a Glance: Specs, Parts & Essentials

2026-10-22 · BeyHK

CX-17 is the product code for Random Booster Vol. 10, the Takara Tomy blind box for Beyblade X that reached Japanese shops on April 25, 2026 at ¥1,600 a box. Every sealed booster holds one complete top drawn from a fixed pool of six, and the pull everyone wants is UnicornDelta PO3-60GU, the balance-type blade whose Triple Style rebuild is the headline gimmick of this volume. Buying blind boxes without knowing the odds burns money fast, and most listings never explain what you are actually paying for. This guide covers the full CX-17 lineup with per-box pull rates, a part-by-part breakdown of the prize Bey, the Triple Style gimmick in plain language, a value verdict for deck builders, and the two routes Hong Kong players can use to get one. Prices stay in the original yen and US dollar figures so you can compare listings without conversion guesswork.

Key takeaways

  • CX-17 is Random Booster Vol. 10: six fixed Beyblade X configurations, one per box, released in Japan on April 25, 2026 at ¥1,600.
  • The prize pull, UnicornDelta PO3-60GU, appears 3 times in every sealed 24-box carton, roughly 1 in 8 boxes.
  • UnicornDelta is a Balance type whose Peak over blade flips the same build between attack, balance and defense setups.
  • Three of the six CX-17 configurations carry the GU (Gear Unite) bit, so even non-prize pulls add useful parts to a parts box.
  • No launcher or stadium is included; both are required to battle and sold separately.

What is CX-17?

CX-17 is Random Booster Vol. 10, a blind box product in the Custom Line (CX) of Takara Tomy's Beyblade X. Released in Japan on April 25, 2026 for ¥1,600, each box holds one of six fixed Beyblades, and the rare prize is UnicornDelta PO3-60GU.

The release was covered at launch by Impress Watch's hobby desk, which lists the April 25, 2026 date and the ¥1,600 price. Random boosters work as a lottery: Takara Tomy customizes six Beys in special colours, seals one complete top per box, and the buyer cannot choose which one lands inside. What makes the CX series different from the older BX and UX lines is the blade itself.

CX stands for Custom Line (CX), the product family whose blades separate into four pieces: a lock chip, an over blade, a metal blade and an assist blade. That four-part stack gives CX releases more combination points than the three-part BX tops, which is why CX-17 matters to builders even beyond the prize pull. If you are new to how blade codes read, a parts guide will help before you open your first box.

The six Beyblades inside CX-17

CX-17 contains six Beys: two UnicornDelta builds and four recolour releases of tops that already existed in the Beyblade X line. Each box holds exactly one of them, and factory cartons pack 24 boxes with a fixed split between the six. Pull rates and the carton math

A factory-sealed master carton holds 24 individual boosters grouped into four bags of six, and the documented split is 3, 3, 4, 5, 4 and 5 units of the six Beys. Dividing by 24 gives the realistic odds: the prize UnicornDelta and the white UnicornDelta each land in 12.5 percent of boxes, or about 1 in 8; the two five-unit commons (HellsHammer and CrimsonGaruda) reach about 1 in 5; the four-unit Beys sit at 1 in 6.

Takara Tomy does not print per-box odds on the packaging, so these figures are carton averages, not guarantees for a single box bought loose. Sellers who crack cartons and resell picked boxes can break the pattern entirely. If you want the documented odds to hold, buy from a sealed carton or split one with friends: 24 buyers sharing a carton each pay for exactly a 1-in-8 shot at the prize, with no skew. Buying a whole carton yourself guarantees three prize pulls plus three of the second UnicornDelta build.

UnicornDelta PO3-60GU: the prize Bey

UnicornDelta PO3-60GU is the prize Beyblade of CX-17 and the reason this volume sold through quickly. It is a Balance type, right-spin, and weighs 46.3 grams assembled. In the toy line it succeeds UnicornSting 5-60GP, and Hasbro later released the same design internationally as Delta Unicorn PO 3-60GU, a starter pack listing at USD$14.99. The name decodes the build: Unicorn is the lock chip, P is the Peak over blade, O is the Odd assist blade, 3-60 is the ratchet, and GU is the Gear Unite bit.

Part Piece in this build Role
Lock chip Unicorn Holds the CX blade stack together and carries the unicorn motif of the series
Over blade Peak A pointed layer that concentrates weight into a single area; its orientation sets the build's style
Metal blade Delta The main metal layer, with two differently shaped blade faces for different jobs
Assist blade Odd An asymmetric sub-blade mixing angled contact points with a curved deflection edge
Ratchet 3-60 Three contact points at 6.0 mm height, a compact mid-height setup
Bit GU (Gear Unite) An all-round bit; the same bit ships on three of the six CX-17 pulls

Triple Style: one Bey, three builds

Triple Style is the gimmick that separates this prize from a standard recolour. Impress Watch's launch report describes UnicornDelta as a balance type that rebuilds into attack, balance or defense depending on which way the over blade faces. Flipping the Peak over blade on the Delta metal blade shifts where the weight sits, so the same handful of parts moves between an aggressive contact profile, a deflecting profile and a mixed profile.

Two practical notes follow from that design. First, the switch is a reassembly, not a mid-battle dial: you rebuild the top between matches, so plan the style before you launch. Second, every rebuild is a chance to seat the lock incorrectly, and a badly seated CX stack can pop apart in battle. Re-check the lock after each style change.

Why deck builders want what is inside

Even a box without the prize adds value, because three of the six configurations ship the GU (Gear Unite) bit: the prize build, HellsHammer 3-85GU and CrimsonGaruda 7-80GU. That is eleven GU bits across a 24-box carton, which makes CX-17 one of the cheapest ways to stock a part that fits several deck roles.

The volume also hides a second UnicornDelta. The white and pink Another Color version can be assembled from the parts of the 02, 05 and 06 pulls, so three common boxes rebuild into the extra build without ever touching the prize. Collectors who want the complete CX-17 set therefore chase fewer boxes than the six-code lineup suggests.

Is CX-17 worth buying for parts and play?

CX-17 is worth buying if you want GU bits, want UnicornDelta, or enjoy the lottery itself, and it is a poor deal if you need one specific common top. The blind box format cuts both ways, so the honest answer depends on which of those you are.

It makes sense when:

  • You want the prize: a 1-in-8 carton rate beats most aftermarket premiums once the volume sells out.
  • You want parts: three of six configurations carry the GU bit, and the ratchets (3-60, 1-80, 9-65, 3-85, 7-80) refill several heights.
  • You want recolours of proven tops such as SamuraiSaber, TyrannoBeat and HellsHammer in colours the standard line never shipped.
  • You enjoy the pull: the fixed carton split makes the odds knowable instead of pure gambling.

Skip it when:

  • You need one specific common build, because singles cost less than a stack of boxes in the long run.
  • You already own the earlier versions of those four recolour Beys and do not care about colourways.
  • Duplicate pulls frustrate you, since HellsHammer and CrimsonGaruda together fill ten of every 24 boxes.

One risk deserves its own line: the odds above describe factory-sealed cartons, and no official per-box rate exists. Loose singles from split cartons can skew any direction, so treat every loose-box purchase as a coin you cannot weigh.

How to get CX-17 in Hong Kong

Hong Kong players have two practical routes: import the Japanese blind box, or buy the Hasbro international version of the prize Bey. Both end at the same design, so the choice is about price, packaging and whether you want a launcher in the same purchase.

  1. Decide between the Japanese box and the Hasbro starter. The Japanese CX-17 is the blind box with the teal and purple prize colourway and no launcher; the Hasbro Delta Unicorn PO 3-60GU CX Infinity Starter Pack is a guaranteed single Bey with a launcher for USD$14.99.
  2. For the Japanese box, check Japanese hobby exporters and proxy buying services, and use the ¥1,600 MSRP as your anchor. After sellout, resale prices climb, so compare against the singles market before paying a premium.
  3. Watch the release calendar for the next waves, because CX-18 (Brachio Whip Select), CX-19 (Crocotread Select) and Random Booster Vol. 11 (BX-50) followed CX-17, and restock or reprint news usually shows up there first. The Beyblade X release calendar tracks every Takara Tomy product by date.
  4. Consider a group order. Splitting one sealed 24-box carton among 24 players reproduces the documented odds exactly, and everyone pays import shipping once instead of 24 times.

The Hasbro route carries its own timing note: the international starter reached Canada on August 17, 2026, and regional availability keeps rolling out, so check local retailers rather than assuming a single global date.

Conclusion

CX-17 rewards buyers who understand what a random booster actually is: a parts lottery with a published carton structure, not a guaranteed product. The number that should drive your decision is the prize split, because UnicornDelta PO3-60GU fills 3 of every 24 factory boxes, so a per-box price close to 1-in-8 odds is fair value and anything above it is premium. Before you buy more than two boxes, price a single UnicornDelta on the secondary market; if one loose Bey costs less than three boxes, buy the single instead. Frequently asked questions

How much does the CX-17 random booster cost?

Japanese MSRP is ¥1,600 per box, tax included, set at the April 25, 2026 launch. That anchor erodes after sellout: importers and resellers price CX-17 by which Beys are still in demand, so expect higher prices on the secondary market. The Hasbro international alternative, the Delta Unicorn PO 3-60GU CX Infinity Starter Pack, lists at USD$14.99 and includes a launcher.

What are the odds of pulling UnicornDelta PO3-60GU from CX-17?

Derived from the documented sealed-carton split, a prize UnicornDelta lands in 3 of every 24 factory boxes, about 12.5 percent or 1 in 8. Takara Tomy does not print per-box odds on the packaging, so this is a carton average rather than a guarantee. Splitting one sealed carton among 24 buyers reproduces those odds exactly.

How do you switch UnicornDelta between attack, balance and defense?

You rebuild the top between battles. Flipping the orientation of the Peak over blade on the Delta metal blade moves the same parts between attack, balance and defense setups, the Triple Style design of this release. It is a mechanical reassembly, not a mid-battle switch, so set the style before the match and re-check the lock after every rebuild.

Does CX-17 include a launcher or stadium?

No. The box holds one complete Beyblade only, and a Beyblade X launcher plus stadium are required to battle, both sold separately as with other random boosters. If you want a launcher in the same purchase, the Hasbro Delta Unicorn starter pack is the version that ships with one.

Is UnicornDelta PO3-60GU good for tournaments?

Its official classification is Balance type with three switchable styles, which makes it a flexible deck piece rather than a specialist. Competitive value shifts as the metagame moves, so treat the triple-style flexibility as the selling point and check your event's current parts rules before locking a deck, since CX-era parts face event-by-event legality calls in some organized play circuits.

What is the difference between CX-17 and the Hasbro Delta Unicorn pack?

CX-17 is the Japanese blind box: one random top from six configurations, no launcher included. The Hasbro product is a guaranteed Delta Unicorn PO 3-60GU sold as a starter pack with a launcher for USD$14.99. Collectors chasing the teal and purple Takara Tomy colourway need the Japanese box; players who just want the design can take the Hasbro route.
