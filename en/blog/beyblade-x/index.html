Inside Beyblade X: Specs, Components & Design Details

2026-10-27 · BeyHK

Beyblade X parts and scoring decide every battle before you even launch, and this guide explains how the system actually works. You will get the three-part structure, how codes like 3-60F decode into parts, what each product line offers, the official point system, real prices in yen, and the mistakes that cost new players money. Everything here comes from Takara Tomy's own materials and established community resources.

Key takeaways:

  • Every Beyblade X top is built from exactly three swappable parts: a Blade, a Ratchet and a Bit.
  • The code on the box decodes the top: in Dran Sword 3-60F, the Ratchet is 3 contact points at 6.0 mm and the Bit letter F means Flat.
  • Official regulation play is first to 4 points: an Xtreme Finish awards 3 points, Over and Burst Finishes award 2, and a Spin Finish awards 1.
  • Beyblade X launched on July 15, 2023 as the fourth Beyblade generation, and its parts interchange across the BX, UX and CX lines.

What is Beyblade X?

Beyblade X is the fourth generation of Takara Tomy's Beyblade battling top system, launched in Japan on July 15, 2023, where every top is assembled from three interchangeable metal-contact parts and battles end in four scored finish types.

Generation four replaced the plastic-on-plastic clashes of Burst with metal contact points and a gear-driven acceleration system. Takara Tomy positions the line as faster and more technical than its predecessors, and the community databases track its launch on July 15, 2023 as the start of ranked generation play. The system was designed to reward timing and angles rather than raw launch power.

The three-part system: Blade, Ratchet, Bit

Every top consists of exactly three parts, and each one has a distinct job. The community parts guide at BladeDex breaks down all three roles, and the logic holds across every product in the line.

  • Blade: the large outer layer that carries most of the weight and does the hitting. Its shape sets the battle type: sharp contact edges for Attack, wide walls for Defense, smooth curves for Stamina.
  • Ratchet: the middle lock that connects Blade to Bit. It sets the top's height and the number of burst-resistance contact points.
  • Bit: the tip that touches the stadium floor. Its shape decides movement and stamina, and on many Bits the underside carries gear teeth that drive the Xtreme Dash.

This three-part stack is why one lost part rarely ruins a whole top. You can swap a single worn Bit and the combo keeps its identity.

Why the part code on the box matters

Reading the code before buying tells you how a top will move, where it will strike and whether it fits your style. The official Beyblade X FAQ confirms the code format: the Ratchet number gives contact points and height in millimetres, and the trailing letter names the Bit.

Take Dran Sword 3-60F. The Ratchet is "3-60": 3 contact points at 6.0 mm high. The trailing letter F is the Bit: Flat. A taller code like 9-80 raises the Blade and changes where two tops strike each other, which the BladeDex height guide explains with examples. Lower Ratchets tend to hit the opponent low; higher ones lift the contact point and can catch taller opponents differently.

How the X-Celerator Rail and Xtreme Dash work

The X-Celerator Rail is the defining gimmick of this generation, and it produces the burst of speed every highlight clip shows. The MonkeeMods Xtreme Line guide explains the mechanism: gear teeth on the Bit's underside engage the rail molded into the stadium, and that mechanical bite rockets the top forward.

Three details matter for new players. First, the dash depends on the Bit's design, not on launch strength, because the gears either mesh or they do not. Second, only Bits with the gear teeth, such as Flat and Gear types, can dash; smooth-tipped stamina Bits never engage the rail. Third, a regular Burst-generation stadium has no rail, so the mechanic simply does not exist there.

This is why attack players favour rail-riding Bits. The dash converts stadium geometry into free acceleration, and it is the difference between a slow drift toward the centre and a sudden line into the opponent's blade.

The three product lines: BX, UX and CX

Beyblade X splits into three product lines, and the code prefix tells you which one you are holding. The line overview on MonkeeMods matches what retailers stock.

Line Code Structure Who it suits
Basic Line BX Standard three-part stack: Blade, Ratchet, Bit Beginners and most competitive play
Unique Line UX Unconventional Blade shapes and built-in gimmicks Players chasing specific matchups
Custom Line CX Modular system: Lock Chip, Main Blade, Assist Blade Deep-customisation hobbyists

Parts stay cross-compatible across lines: a UX Blade fits a BX Ratchet and Bit, and every launcher and stadium works with all of them. That compatibility is the quiet strength of the system, because a collection grows without orphaning older parts.

How battles are scored

Official regulation play uses a first-to-4 match, and the finish types are worth different amounts. The current Takara Tomy Asia regulation PDF (6th edition, November 2024) defines the four finishes, and the community tournament guide confirms the standard point values.

Finish Points What happens
Xtreme Finish 3 The opponent's top exits through the Xtreme Zone at the stadium's centre front
Over Finish 2 The opponent's top leaves the stadium through an Over Zone pocket
Burst Finish 2 The opponent's top detaches into parts mid-battle
Spin Finish 1 The opponent's top stops spinning first

Two edge cases decide close matches. Simultaneous finishes count as a draw and the battle restarts, per the regulation's simultaneous-finish clause. And knocking yourself out without contact hands the opponent a reduced score under the community's Own Finish rule.

How to start collecting Beyblade X

  1. Buy one Starter first. Starters are the only tier that includes a launcher. The first wave covered BX-01 through BX-04: Dran Sword, Hells Scythe, Wizard Arrow and Knight Shield.
  2. Add a stadium. The dash mechanic needs the X-Celerator Rail, so pick a stadium with the Xtreme Line molded in. A Burst stadium will not trigger it.
  3. Learn to read box codes. Blade name plus Ratchet points-height plus Bit letter tells you the playstyle before you buy.
  4. Assemble by hand. Blade on top, Ratchet in the middle, Bit below. No tools needed.
  5. Play first-to-4 matches and log your results so you can see which matchups your combo loses.

Common beginner mistakes with Beyblade X

Mistake Why it hurts Fix
Buying a Random Booster as your first product Boosters ship parts-only with no launcher, so one Booster alone cannot battle Buy a Starter first, Boosters second
Ignoring the Ratchet height Height decides where your Blade strikes, so a mismatched height loses trades Match height to your Blade's contact angle
Using a Burst stadium No Xtreme Line rail means no dash mechanic at all Buy a stadium with the rail molded in
Reusing one part across all three Beys in 3-on-3 Official deck rules forbid repeating a part across your deck Build the deck around three distinct part sets

The deck rule comes from official 3-on-3 play: the same part cannot appear more than once across your three tops, and that single rule shapes most deck-building decisions.

Pros, cons, risks and limitations

  • Strength: every part is interchangeable, so one purchase upgrades every combo you own.
  • Strength: the rail mechanic adds a skill layer that previous generations lacked.
  • Limitation: the mechanic needs specific stadiums and Bits, which raises the true entry cost.
  • Limitation: older Burst-era products cannot compete in official X play.
  • Risk: counterfeits circulate widely, and Takara Tomy has published warnings about non-official products.

Buy from licensed retailers and treat prices far below the official line as a warning sign. The official product pages also note that Takara Tomy has taken measures against fraudulent activity around the brand.

The through-line across four generations is simple: each one changed how tops touch each other. X changed it with metal at height and speed.

Beyblade X rewards players who read the system before they spend: the three-part structure means one Starter, one rail stadium and a couple of extra parts cover most of what a beginner needs. Frequently asked questions

What does 3-60F mean on a Beyblade X box?

It decodes the top's parts. The Ratchet is 3-60: 3 contact points at 6.0 mm high, and the trailing letter F names the Bit as Flat, which gives fast, aggressive movement. The official FAQ documents this format, and the numbers stay consistent across every product in the line.

How much does a Beyblade X Starter cost?

Japanese Takara Tomy Starters have sat in the ¥1,500 to ¥2,700 range across the line's first waves, varying by Blade size and included bits, and prices in your local market depend on the importer. [VERIFY: current official pricing page] Boosters cost less but include no launcher, so they only make sense as a second purchase.

Can Beyblade X parts mix between the BX, UX and CX lines?

Yes. Blades, Ratchets, Bits, launchers and stadiums interchange across all three lines, with a small number of CX tops integrating parts more deeply. The one rule that limits mixing is the 3-on-3 deck rule: the same part cannot repeat across your three tops in official play.

What is the Xtreme Dash?

It is a burst of acceleration triggered when a gear-toothed Bit engages the X-Celerator Rail molded into the stadium floor. The engagement is mechanical, so it depends on the Bit's design rather than launch power alone. Only Bits with the teeth, such as Flat and Gear types, can ride the rail.

Are old Beyblades compatible with Beyblade X?

No. Official play only recognises Beyblade X tops, launchers and stadiums, and Burst, Metal Fight and original-generation products are prohibited at sanctioned events. The parts dimensions changed with generation four, so old parts also physically do not assemble into an X top.

What are the risks of buying Beyblade X online?

The main risks are counterfeits and inflated resale prices. Takara Tomy has warned publicly about non-official products and acted against fraud around the brand, so buy from licensed retailers and treat far-below-official prices as a red flag.

How do you win a Beyblade X battle?

Score 4 points before your opponent does. Finishes score differently: an Xtreme Finish through the centre-front zone earns 3 points, Over and Burst Finishes earn 2, and a Spin Finish earns 1. Simultaneous finishes restart the battle as a draw.
