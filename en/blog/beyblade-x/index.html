Inside Beyblade X: Specs, Components & Design Details

2026-10-22 · BeyHK

Beyblade X is Takara Tomy's fourth-generation battling top system, launched in Japan on 15 July 2023. Every top in the system is built from three swappable parts — Blade, Ratchet and Bit — and the part code tells you the build: a Ratchet like 3-60 means 3 contact points and a height of 6.0 mm, while the trailing letter picks the Bit, so Dran Sword 3-60F runs a Flat tip. What sets this generation apart is the X-Celerator Rail in the stadium, which meshes with the gear teeth on the Bit to trigger the Xtreme Dash — a burst of speed unique to Beyblade X. Official matches are played to 4 points: an Xtreme Finish earns 3, an Over/Knockout Finish and a Burst Finish earn 2 each, and a Spin (Survivor) Finish earns 1. And since parts are cross-compatible across the BX, UX and CX lines, one purchase grows your options instead of locking you in.

What is Beyblade X?

Beyblade X is a competitive spinning-top system where every top is assembled from three separate parts, a Blade on top, a Ratchet in the middle and a Bit at the tip, and battles are decided by knockouts, bursts or spin time inside a stadium with an acceleration rail.

Takara Tomy positions it as the fourth generation: original Beyblade from 1999, Metal Fight from 2008, Burst from 2015, and X from July 2023 [2]. The series also marks the brand's 25th anniversary, which is why the launch line leaned on remakes of fan-favourite blades alongside new designs.

The three-part system

  • Blade: the large, heavy outer layer that carries the artwork and most of the mass. Its shape sets the battle type: sharp contact edges for Attack, wide walls for Defense, smooth curves for Stamina [3].
  • Ratchet: the middle lock that connects Blade to Bit. It sets the top's height and the number of burst-resistance contact points [3].
  • Bit: the tip that touches the stadium. Its shape decides movement and stamina, and on many Bits the underside carries gear teeth that drive the Xtreme Dash [4].

How to read a part code like 3-60F

The Ratchet number gives the contact points and the height in tenths of a millimetre. A 3-60 Ratchet has 3 locking clicks and sits 6.0 mm tall; 4-80 means 4 clicks at 8.0 mm. The trailing letter is the Bit: F is Flat, T is Taper, B is Ball, N is Needle and so on [5]. So the launch-line Dran Sword 3-60F is a Dran Sword Blade on a 3-point, 6.0 mm Ratchet with a Flat, aggressive tip. Higher Ratchet numbers generally mean a taller top that is harder to under-cut; more contact points generally mean better burst resistance.

Why the X-Celerator Rail matters

The rail is the generation's signature. Gear teeth cut into the Bit mesh with teeth lining the stadium's Xtreme Line, and the stadium's geometry mechanically drives the top forward into a sudden burst of speed that Takara Tomy calls the Xtreme Dash [4]. Because the effect comes from physical tooth engagement, not friction, it only triggers when the Bit's gears actually catch the rail, which is why Flat and Gear-tipped Bits dominate attack play. Launch-line tops sold for ¥1,980 each bundled with a winder launcher, which made the barrier to entry unusually low for a competitive system [1].

The three product lines: BX, UX and CX

Parts are cross-compatible across lines: a UX Blade fits a BX Ratchet and Bit, and every launcher and stadium works with all of them [3]. One practical note: the BX-08 3-on-3 Deck Set at ¥3,960 is the cheapest way to get three complete Beys at once, which official 3-on-3 rules require [6].

How battles are scored

Official regulation play uses a 4-point match [7]:

  • Xtreme Finish (opponent sent into the Xtreme Zone): 3 points
  • Over/Knockout Finish (opponent forced out into the Over Zone): 2 points
  • Burst Finish (opponent's parts detach): 2 points
  • Spin/Survivor Finish (opponent stops spinning first): 1 point

Competitive matches use three Beys per deck in 3-on-3 battle, and the same part cannot appear more than once across the three tops, even in different colours [7]. This deck-building rule is why part cross-compatibility matters so much in practice.

Pros, cons, risks and limitations

  • Pros: three-part customisation across every line, a low entry price for a complete Starter, and a skill ceiling raised by the rail mechanic.
  • Cons: Random Booster products ship parts-only with no launcher, so a first Booster alone does not get you battling [6]. CX's extra layer adds cost and complexity that beginners do not need yet.
  • Limitations: only genuine Beyblade X parts are legal in official play; disassembly, modification and performance-changing paint are prohibited, and judges disqualify offenders [7]. Products from older generations are not usable at official events.
  • Risk for buyers: scalping and counterfeit products are common enough that Takara Tomy has published public warnings about non-official products, so buy from licensed retailers [8].

How to get started with Beyblade X

  1. Buy one Starter (BX-01 to BX-04 at launch: Dran Sword, Hells Scythe, Wizard Arrow, Knight Shield). A Starter is the only tier that includes a launcher [6].
  2. Add a Beyblade X stadium. The Xtreme Line rail is required for the dash mechanic; regular Burst stadiums will not engage it [4].
  3. Learn to read the code on the box so you know the type before battling: Blade name + Ratchet points-height + Bit letter [5].
  4. Assemble by hand: Blade on top, Ratchet in the middle, Bit underneath. No tools required.
  5. Battle to 4 points using the official scoring, and track your results so you can see which matchups your combo loses [7].

Frequently asked questions

What does a code like 3-60F mean?

The Ratchet number shows contact points and height in tenths of a millimetre, and the letter is the Bit. A 3-60F is a 3-click, 6.0 mm Ratchet with a Flat tip [5]. Taller numbers sit higher; more clicks resist bursts better.

Which Beyblade X line should a beginner buy?

Start with BX, the Basic Line. It is the cheapest complete system, uses the standard three-part stack, and its parts work with every other line later [5]. CX makes sense once you want deeper customisation.

How much does a Beyblade X Starter cost?

On Takara Tomy's Japanese line a Starter runs ¥1,980 to ¥2,750 depending on the line and box size, and includes a launcher. Random Boosters cost around ¥1,400 to ¥1,600 but contain parts only [6].

Is Beyblade X compatible with older Beyblade generations?

No. Only Beyblade X tops, launchers and stadiums are legal in official play; Burst, Metal Fight and original generation products are prohibited at sanctioned events [7].

Can I mix parts from different Beyblade X lines?

Yes. Blades, Ratchets, Bits, launchers and stadiums are cross-compatible across BX, UX and CX, with a small number of newer tops integrating the Ratchet into the Blade or Bit [3]. The one deck rule to remember: no part may repeat across your three Beys in official 3-on-3 play [7].

What is the Xtreme Dash?

It is the acceleration burst triggered when gear teeth on the Bit mesh with the stadium's Xtreme Line rail. The engagement is mechanical, so it depends on the Bit's design rather than launch power alone [4].

What are the risks of buying Beyblade X online?

Counterfeits and scalped prices are the main risks. Takara Tomy publishes notices about non-official products and has taken measures against fraudulent activity around the brand [8]. Buy from licensed retailers and treat unusually cheap listings as a warning sign.

Conclusion

The single most useful insight for a new player: read the part code before you buy, because the Ratchet height and Bit letter tell you how a top will behave long before you launch it. Your concrete next step is to pick one BX Starter that matches your style, Flat Bit for attack, Needle or Ball for defense and stamina, and battle a friend to 4 points using the official scoring.
